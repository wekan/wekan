# Aromanian color terminology review

Reviewed **2026-09-14**. `color-magenta` remains pending. Its local value
`magenta` may be a loanword; identical English spelling alone establishes
neither an error nor correct Aromanian usage.

The Society Farsharotu's [English–Aromanian dictionary](https://s3.wasabisys.com/fars-media/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/An-English-Aromanian-Macedo-Romanian-Dictionary-%C2%A9Society-Farsharotu-1.pdf)
was inspected at PDF page 445 (zero-based page 444), where the alphabetical
entries proceed from magician to magnet. No magenta entry occurs in that
sequence. This is stronger than an unsuccessful OCR keyword search, but
does not establish that modern speakers cannot use the loanword. The scan's
OCR frequently misreads English headings; other terminology still requires
page inspection or an independent native source.

Chivarzina's [2023 Aromanian color field study](https://ezikovsvyat.swu.bg/images/stories/issue%2021.2_2023/8.%20A.Chivarzina_61_66.pdf)
combines dictionary evidence with interviews in Bitola and Krushevo.
Pages 63 and 65 discuss wine-derived words covering blue and purple shades,
and the limited secondary-color evidence obtained in interviews. Those
findings do not justify replacing local `color-purple` with a supposedly
exclusive blue term, or substituting pink/purple for magenta. The study
also supports the native red term already present as `color-red`.

Next: locate an independently attested Aromanian magenta name or a native
technical color glossary. Romanian dictionary entries are not Aromanian
attestation. Preserve the existing value until that evidence establishes
the correct repair or retention. No acceptance, replacement, or browser
verification is claimed by this research; the Aromanian finding remains pending.
